## Recommendation: Reborn as a Fantasy General (Army-Building Isekai)
- Royal Road original, ONGOING, human male MC (Marcus Graham).
- Current page: ~183 chapters / ~1,333 RR pages; Books 1 and 2 complete; normal update schedule listed as Tuesday/Friday.
- No System; magic exists but is relatively rare. Main progression is strategic/political rather than personal martial dominance.
- Premise begins on modern Earth for roughly the opening setup, then moves into a fantasy war setting. This is a caveat against the user's strong fantasy-first preference.
- Opening chapter inspected. It is the weakest part for this user: very on-the-nose modern ideological/campus debate, and some reviewers explicitly recommend pushing past/through it. Marcus is hotheaded and recognizes that he lets himself get baited; he is not presented as flawless.
- Once in the fantasy world, Marcus is physically ordinary/vulnerable. His advantage is military-history knowledge rather than secret combat talent, and reviews explicitly note that he is not expected to become a martial force.
- Strong evidence of actual setbacks: later material shows opponents can unravel strategic preparations and destroy resources/plans that took weeks to build; later chapters explicitly show Marcus learning from prior failures and adapting. Campaigns also impose real costs rather than resetting cleanly. Keep user-facing discussion high-level/no spoilers.
- Strong Hundred Reigns-adjacent signals: multiple fantasy races/factions with their own kingdoms and motivations; shifting POVs; political intrigue; reviews praise breadcrumbed plot threads that form before major events, leaving the reader waiting to see how the different cards/players collide.
- Reviews also praise actual character motivations and the fact that plans do not simply work because the MC is smart.
- Major caveats: military/army-building is the core; less magic/mystery than Hundred Reigns; the ratfolk initially treat Marcus as a prophesied/savior-like figure, which risks early validation, though his actual competence remains limited to knowledge and he can fail. The stylized ratfolk dialogue can also grate. One critical review at ch80 found Marcus self-pitying/insufferable.
- Overall: stronger fit for the repeated-setback requirement than for the mystical 'major figures collide' hype signal.

## Wildcard: Gods Die in Silence [Dark Fantasy | Steampunk | Isekai]
- Royal Road original, ONGOING, human male MC Damian, currently ~79-81 listed chapters / ~534-545 pages. Latest TOC showed chapters within the last day; actively updating.
- No AI-Assisted/Generated warning surfaced.
- Dark steampunk religious high fantasy rather than medieval fantasy; major setting caveat.
- Damian is already an Inquisition agent and confidant of an Imperial Princess at age 17. He is not a fake-weak nobody, so this avoids the 'F-rank secretly equal to prodigy' setup, but it begins with him already unusually connected/statusful.
- Opening three chapters inspected directly. Prose is atmospheric, first-person, and action-heavy. It has some grammar/typo roughness but did not read like the obvious AI-flavored material previously rejected. The MC begins competent but is immediately capable of being physically overwhelmed; his first assignment shown is substantially more dangerous than expected and forces him to question going solo.
- Strongest fit is the user's newly stated Hundred Reigns 'hype' signal: layered religious/divine hierarchy, massive political institutions, secret identities, cults/conspiracies, gods and ancient figures whose scale makes Damian feel small, and reviews repeatedly praise the sense that separate mysteries/threads are building toward large payoffs.
- Current reviews say the world unfolds organically, the political/religious structure feels lived-in, side characters have clashing/evolving motives, and later reveals feel earned. One review specifically describes the mystery/politics/cults/divine conspiracies clicking together piece by piece until the scale becomes much larger than expected.
- Evidence for recurring *clean losses* is weaker than for Reborn as a Fantasy General. Later material does continue to show Damian being outclassed, pressured by more powerful figures, and learning the hard way, but current evidence does not justify claiming Re:Zero/Hundred Reigns-level repeated objective failure. Surface as a 'hype/layered world' wildcard, not a perfect answer to the loss criterion.
- Author notes that an older Morren Arc is rough/noncanonical and recommends starting from Arc 1 / 'The Nightlurker Plague'; current fiction page uses that as chapter 1.
