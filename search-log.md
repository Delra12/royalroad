## 2026-09-25 — clarified failure criterion

### Key insight
The user does **not** require a weak protagonist. An OP protagonist is fine. The important requirement is that the protagonist can genuinely lose/fail repeatedly.

Time-loop stories are especially strong because death can function as a real failure state without ending the narrative. Preferred failures involve being outplayed, making a wrong decision, failing an objective, losing a fight/person/opportunity, or discovering that an approach was fundamentally wrong. The best progression from a loss is information/understanding and a changed strategy, not simply more stats.

### Discussed in this search
- A Regressor’s Tale of Cultivation — strongest fit from that search; already used now.
- Purple Days — potentially strong fit; fanfic wildcard; already used now.
- Death After Death — genuine repeated failure, but early MC may be irritating; already used now.
- Dear Spellbook — mechanics fit, presentation may not; already used now.
- The Undying Immortal System — repeated failure but potentially too cultivation/crafting-grind heavy; already used now.
- DIE. RESPAWN. REPEAT. — rejected because death increasingly functions as XP/power farming rather than meaningful failure.

### Rule for future searches
Search for evidence of repeated meaningful loss/failure, not merely tags like “time loop,” “regression,” or “weak-to-strong.” A title where the MC technically dies but each death is just an optimization mechanic is not necessarily a fit.

## 2026-09-26 — medieval setting + Re:ZERO-style failure loop search

### Updated target
User clarified that Re:ZERO is structurally close to what they want: repeated catastrophic failure, death/reset, wrong assumptions, information gain, and genuinely bad runs. They dislike the anime/light-novel trope-heavy presentation. They have also read The Hedge Wizard; they thought it was decent, but it is not the target.

### Strong candidate: Player 0.4 [You have died.] [Reset in progress.]
- Royal Road, male MC, ongoing, roughly 290 chapters, weekly release schedule.
- Starts and remains in a medieval/high-fantasy kingdom context: castles, nobles, war, magic, houses, politics, assassinations, etc.
- The opening begins at the catastrophic fall of the Adovorian Kingdom; Luca dies in a lost war and resets seven years.
- The author explicitly designed it against the common regressor pattern where future knowledge instantly turns a loser into an unstoppable genius.
- Later reviews are unusually aligned with the user's target: progression is described as primarily **information-driven**, with Luca growing through knowledge of pitfalls, hidden plots, and which allies can be trusted rather than stat grinding.
- Reviews mention hidden history/lore, shifting character relationships, other actors with their own loop-related knowledge, politics/shadow games, and behind-the-scenes mysteries.
- System/GameLit wrapper is the main caveat. However, reviews describe the LitRPG layer as minimal, and the actual opening prose reads like straightforward Western web fantasy rather than anime/light-novel banter.
- Second caveat: Luca is intentionally not very intelligent early and can make frustrating decisions. Some critical reviews complain about lack of agency or him failing to exploit the loop optimally. This may either be a feature (real failure) or an irritation for the user.

### Strong/risky candidate: The Skeleton Soldier Failed to Defend the Dungeon
- Korean web novel, licensed English translation on Wuxiaworld; roughly 401 English chapters available, listed at 7 chapters/week.
- Dark medieval-fantasy setting from the beginning. Male/nonhuman MC (nameless skeleton).
- Core mechanic is return-by-death. He repeatedly fails to protect people, dies, returns, changes approach, and uncovers the world's hidden nature.
- Official synopsis explicitly frames repeated downfall as revealing the world's true nature. Translator notes emphasize growth in both strength and intelligence, nobles/wealthy rulers as major threats, political/city intrigue, twists, and forbidden truths that spiral empires into war.
- Strong evidence of genuine repeated failure: early arcs contain one-sided defeats by foes he cannot beat, resets that alter starting points/rules, and later resets can strip away previously trusted power.
- Caveats: prominent status-window/system mechanics, Korean translated-webnovel flavor, and the protagonist is a skeleton rather than a human noble/student. This is likely less anime-comedy than Re:ZERO but still more overtly game-system flavored than the user's ideal.

### Risky / not primary recommendation: Master of the Loop
- Complete, 210 chapters. Medieval/high-fantasy portal setting, male MC, save-point death loop.
- Early portion has explored paths, brutal failure, mystery, and an overarching plot.
- Reviews say the first ~50–100 chapters are the strongest and fit time-loop fantasy well.
- Major reason not to prioritize: later reviews complain that the story spends roughly a hundred chapters in one loop, loses focus on looping, and lets the MC become very OP. Some also find the MC frustratingly dumb. This weakens the exact sustained-failure-loop structure the user wants.

- **God's Loop: Dying Endlessly in Search of a Perfect Ending** — inactive.

### Search lesson
The user is not merely asking for “time loop fantasy.” The closest target is now: **medieval/high-fantasy home setting + male MC + repeated catastrophic failure that remains plausible across many arcs + information/relationship/political knowledge as progression + grounded prose/tone + minimal anime tropes + no death-as-XP shortcut.**

## 2026-09-26 — human-MC-only refinement

### New hard constraint
- Human male MC only. Nonhuman protagonists are now a hard mismatch. This specifically rules out Skeleton Soldier despite its otherwise strong loop structure.
- User rejected Player 0.4 as well; do not resurface it.

### Strong candidate: A Knight Who Eternally Regresses / The Knight Only Lives Today
- Human male MC: Encrid/Enkrid.
- Core setting is explicitly medieval European-style fantasy: mercenaries, knights, armies, nobility, war, magic, elves, assassins, territory management.
- He dies in battle and resets to the beginning of the same day. He can remain trapped on one day for many deaths until he learns enough, trains enough, or discovers the right approach to survive.
- Evidence from later chapters/reviews shows repeated genuine failure remains central, including repeated deaths to opponents/events that he cannot initially overcome. This is not a one-off regression premise.
- Progression is largely retained skill/experience/knowledge rather than a stat reward on death.
- Supporting cast is praised; MC continues to struggle and learns from each repeat.
- Strongest caveat: compared with Re:ZERO/Hundred Reigns, it is more combat/training/military focused and appears lighter on puzzle-box mystery/politics.
- Second caveat: it is a Korean web novel, and currently available English prose translations vary in quality. The official English WEBTOON adaptation exists under **The Knight Only Lives Today** and may be the cleaner entry point, but the web novel has far more material.
- Novel Updates lists the source at 2500+ chapters and ongoing, so backlog/activity are excellent.

### Promising but too early: Echoes of Tomorrow: A Time Loop Reckoning
- Royal Road, human male MC Isaac, ongoing.
- Medieval/high-fantasy setting from chapter one: prisoner wagon, swords, soldiers, ghouls, witches, nobles/manors, magic, crown/religious structures.
- MC repeatedly dies and wakes on the same wagon. Current chapters show at least eight loops, with failures revealing enemy behavior, magic rules, character identities/relationships, hidden factions, and routes through events.
- Strong alignment with the desired information-gain loop structure: he explicitly changes tactics based on prior deaths, saves people who died in earlier loops, learns names/motives, and recognizes that people in each timeline are distinct versions whose deaths still matter emotionally.
- Tone/prose is Western-web-serial rather than anime/light-novel flavored.
- Main problem: as of Sept 26, 2026 it only has about 27 chapters / ~547 pages after a recent rewrite/relaunch. The author advertises at least 4 chapters/week, but the backlog is still small and the story is too young to know whether quality/loop structure holds up long-term.

### Rejected / not prioritized in this search
- **No Man's Wish** — human male loop MC, but WWI-style trench-war fantasy rather than medieval; wrong setting.
- **The Crimson Regressions** — premise screams chosen-one/anime-webnovel tropes (Demon King + Goddess Saint parentage, ten Fables, fate modifier); poor tonal fit.
- **Human Lord Summoning** — inactive, AI-assisted, and modern-class-isekai framing.
- **An Infinite Recursion of Time** — explicit harem/GameLit wish-fulfillment orientation; poor fit.
